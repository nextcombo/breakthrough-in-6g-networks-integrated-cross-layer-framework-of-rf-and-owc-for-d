Title: A Cross-Layer Framework for 6G Networks that Integrates RF and OWC, Supporting Dynamic Modulation Scheme Selection
Abstract: With the rapid development of wireless networks, exploring new technologies to meet the needs of future systems such as 6G has become a significant challenge. Integrating radio frequency (RF) and optical wireless communication (OWC) technologies, leveraging their unique advantages, is a highly promising solution. This paper proposes a novel model for integrating RF and OWC technologies suitable for emerging 6G networks, with the core goal of achieving dynamic technology selection (TS) and modulation scheme selection (MSS), thereby optimizing network efficiency and adapting to diverse 6G requirements. The proposed cross-layer architecture integrates the application layer, network layer based on Software-Defined Networking (SDN), and the physical layer composed of hybrid cells and software-defined radios with optical capabilities, enabling real-time decision-making based on environmental factors and application needs.
Introduction: The demand for emerging applications such as digital twins, augmented reality, and vehicle-to-vehicle communication has driven the development of 6G networks. 6G must meet the differentiated performance requirements of various services, including efficient spectrum utilization, high speed, low latency, and high energy efficiency. The performance priorities of different applications vary significantly, and these demands exceed those of the 5G network.
Radio Frequency (RF) technology is central to wireless communications but faces challenges due to the expected increase in connected devices by 2030 to 3 billion, leading to spectrum congestion and increased interference, severely limiting high-speed transmission and performance stability. Optical wireless communication (OWC) offers abundant spectrum resources and can achieve high-speed, ultra-low latency transmission. Combining the long-range coverage advantage of RF with the large capacity advantage of OWC could meet the diverse application needs of 6G, such as full-area connectivity in smart cities and high-density hotspot areas with stadiums covered by OWC.
The core challenge in the integration of RF and OWC is to select appropriate technologies and modulation schemes based on environmental and application scenarios. Currently, most research focuses on the integration of RF and fiber optic systems, with limited literature available on the integration of RF and OWC technologies, especially through dynamic management of novel modulation schemes to achieve their integration. Therefore, this paper proposes an innovative method to integrate the RF and OWC systems in a cross-layer framework that integrates application layer, network layer, and physical layer.
Method Introduction: This paper achieves the integration of RF and OWC through the construction of a cross-layer hybrid architecture, which includes an application layer, network layer, and physical layer. Each layer collaborates to complete technology selection (TS) and modulation scheme selection (MSS) through real-time monitoring and closed-loop decision-making mechanisms. The detailed design is as follows:
1  
Application Layer: Map the needs of various applications in 6G into key performance indicators (KPIs) such as ultra-high data rate, ultra-low latency, and massive connectivity. These KPIs are then used to transform user demands into network strategies, providing a basis for subsequent decisions.
2  
Network Layer: Designing a three-tier architecture based on Software-Defined Networking (SDN) for the application, control, and data planes. The application plane transforms 6G application requirements into strategies such as TS and MSS. The data plane monitors channel states, environmental conditions, network load, etc. Real-time. The SDN controller in the control plane acts as the core, receiving monitoring information and combining it with application layer strategies to make decisions on TS and MSS, while also performing functions such as switching and load balancing, and issuing reconfiguration commands to the data plane.
3  
Physical layer: Design a software-defined radio (SDR-O) with optical functionality, integrate RF antennas and optical transceivers, enable RF/OWC technology switching and modulation scheme adjustment through software reconfiguration, while also responsible for wireless data transmission and providing real-time information such as link quality to the network layer.
Additionally, a closed-loop decision-making mechanism has been established. The SDN controller continuously monitors metrics such as channel status, link quality, network load, environmental conditions, and application KPIs. A weighted scoring model is triggered for vertical/horizontal switching. Different load balancing strategies are designed for stationary and mobile users. At the same time, the time delay boundaries of each decision-making stage are clearly defined to ensure that the end-to-end reconfiguration delay meets the requirements of 6G applications. Through typical scenarios of vehicle-to-vehicle communication, the cross-layer framework's dynamic adaptability has been validated.
The study also conducted performance simulations on the mainstream RF/optical modulation schemes for 6G (OFDM, OTFS, OCDM, SCMA, OFDM-IM and its optical version) under AWGN and multipath channels. Comparative analyses were completed from the perspectives of bit error rate (BER), peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR), and spectral efficiency, providing data support for dynamic modulation scheme selection.
Conclusion: This paper proposes an innovative cross-layer hybrid method that integrates RF and OWC technologies to meet the diverse and high-demand applications of 6G networks. By integrating the application layer, SDN-based network layer, and physical layer with mixed cells and SDR-O, this method achieves dynamic modulation scheme selection, providing flexible and scalable solutions for future wireless networks.
However, the development of this cross-layer hybrid network still faces several challenges that need further research: developing efficient spectrum sharing and resource allocation algorithms to maximize the advantages of RF/OWC integration; expanding security frameworks at all layers to reduce system vulnerabilities; optimizing the power consumption of hybrid transceivers while meeting the high energy consumption demands of real-time network adjustments; establishing standard protocols to ensure interoperability among different technologies, devices, and vendors in the hybrid network; and incorporating advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence and machine learning into the cross-layer framework to predict and adapt to dynamic network conditions. Addressing these challenges will promote the optimization, reliability, and scalability of the cross-layer hybrid network, laying a crucial foundation for meeting the needs of 6G communication systems.
